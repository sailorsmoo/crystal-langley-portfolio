Findings07 notes

Does it repurpose?

  1. The semantic set

    This is the reverse of the Donblas result. Sequoia declares emerald, amber, orange, red and violet alongside the teal and navy core, so up and down never have to borrow a ramp step. The Reports screen above is the proof: plan distribution and coverage read as distinct series without any of them being teal, and the status badge — 22px tall, 3px radius — already carries request and added states across the App Market screens.

  2. The alternating rhythm

    Label block and specimen trading sides down the page costs nothing and reads as editorial rather than dashboard. It is a useful counterweight for a system whose native mode is a dense grid of 196px app cards — the same content, paced slowly, gives one number room to be read.

  3. The radius and shadow language

    The reference's floating cards want 18px corners and a soft triple-layer shadow. Sequoia's card is 5px with a single 0 1px 3px shadow, and its buttons and inputs are 3px. Honoured at source values, as above, the cards read crisp and administrative rather than premium and floating. That is a legitimate look, but it is not the reference's look, and softening it would mean inventing a radius step the system does not have.

  4. The monospace voice

    Same gap as Donblas, same cause. Every eyebrow, legend figure and footnote in the reference is monospace, and that is what makes the layout feel instrumented. Sequoia declares Roboto and Roboto Slab and no mono. The legends above use Roboto Medium with tabular figures, which keeps the columns aligned and loses the instrumented quality. A data face would be a third family — worth it for an admin console, hard to justify for a storefront.

  5. The display specimen, with a caveat

    Setting both families at display size works, and it shows Roboto Slab doing something the product never asks of it. The system's real ceiling is 30px — the stat tile figure — so the specimen is a demonstration of the face, not of a token. The ruler underneath is honest: seven steps, 11px to 30px, and nothing above.

  6. The logo construction plate

    Kept as a frame, but the content does not transfer. The mark is a 36 × 36 organic tree silhouette; there is no underlying construction, so the grid above is illustration rather than derivation, and the labels say what is true — 1:1, 5px, organic, two polarities. Sequoia also has three marks, not one: a dark-ground lockup, a tree, and the icon. A single centred specimen hides that.

  7. The content shape

    Four cards — utilisation, a recommendation, a trend and a variance — is close to the question an IT owner actually opens a marketplace with: what am I paying for, what should I do, is it getting better, am I over. The copy discipline the layout enforces, a two-word headline and a one-line caption, is more valuable here than the visual treatment, because the product's default is to show thirty app cards and no opinion.
