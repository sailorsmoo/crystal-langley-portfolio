The Challenge

The site showed a quarter of the company.

Donblas is auditable AI for litigation. It does not summarize the law, it quantifies it: a probability for every cause of action, with the precedent behind each percentage point. The founders are trial lawyers and ML researchers, and the company is mid seed raise. The product is serious.

The website does not show it. The homepage leads with a stock photo of a city skyline, an all-caps headline, and a single waitlist button. No product, no probability output, no proof. My verdict was blunt: the site shows about a quarter of what Donblas actually is.

01 — Where it is today

A stock skyline, where the product should be.

The homepage opens on a stock photo of office towers, dimmed behind an all-caps headline and a single Join the Waitlist button. The pitch sits low-contrast over the image, hard to read. A company whose brand explicitly rules out stock photography is leading with stock photography. Nowhere in the hero is the actual product: the probability engine, the audit trail, the Privacy Gateway, or the founders who are the moat.

The live site today. Captured from donblas.ai, May 2026.

Stock, not story
Generic office towers for a symbolic reasoning engine. The hero could belong to any bank or brokerage, and the brand's own rules forbid stock photography.
None of the product
No probability output, no audit trail, no Privacy Gateway. The thing that makes Donblas Donblas never appears.
Squint-test copy
The pitch sits low-contrast over a busy photo. A reader has to work to take in the one paragraph that matters.
Waitlist before trust
A single call to action asks for signup before the page has shown a product, a result, or a proof point.
02 — Positioning

Lead with the answer, not the help.

I mapped the field. Legora and Harvey are racing to be the lawyer's assistant, and Legora is far ahead on social proof, positioning itself with lawyers. Donblas should not blur into that helper category, because its wedge is different.

Donblas gives the answer and shows its work: a calibrated probability, an auditable chain of reasoning, and a Privacy Gateway that keeps client data inside the firm. Built by lawyers, for lawyers. The brand has to lead with that, not with another chat box.

The wedge is auditability
A reviewable probability tree, not a black box. The thing that wins motions, made the center of the story.
Privacy Gateway as a claim
The biggest positioning miss on the old site becomes a category line: client data never leaves the firm.
Founders as moat
Esquire and PhD on the same masthead. The combination is the product, and now it is on the page.
Own the comparison
Donblas versus Harvey, Donblas versus Legora. Define the difference instead of defending it.
03 — The direction

From muddy mauve to Mythic Precision.

I set a new visual direction. An obsidian base, forged bronze for the gravitas of the bench, and electric cyan reserved strictly for data. An editorial serif for the argument, a strict monospace for the engine. It should read like a high-end legal instrument, not a SaaS template. This is the direction I am driving the brand toward, away from the navy and mauve "Liberty" system on record.

A high-contrast display serif carries the headlines, the way precedent carries weight. History and authority, set in type.
Monospace for the engine
JetBrains Mono handles every number, label, and panel. It signals the algorithmic, probabilistic core without a single illustration.
Cyan means data, only

A bracketed voice

04 — The homepage

A scrolling argument, not a brochure.

I rebuilt the homepage as six numbered chapters that move from the problem to the doctrine to the products to the engine. It is built in real code, not mockups. Each product shows up as a working panel: the Outcome Predictor returns a probability per cause of action, the Privacy Gateway scrubs and substitutes client entities before anything leaves the firm, and the Trial Trainer runs in an isolated sandbox. Hover any statute in the copy and the odds for that rule, in that court, surface in place.

Outcome Predictor
A live panel: causes of action, the bench filters, and a calibrated probability with the sub-elements that drive it.
Privacy Gateway
A before-and-after scrub. Named parties become deterministic aliases, reversible only inside the firm.
Trial Trainer
An isolated simulation environment, framed as a sandbox terminal. The litigator loses the argument once before it counts.
Auditable citations
Statutes in the body are interrogable. Hover a rule and see what it has been worth, in this court, this year.
05 — Imagery

I rendered the data, I did not photograph it.

The brand rules out stock photography, so I built the imagery instead. These are abstract 3D renders of the reasoning itself: leaf facts converging through nodes into a single auditable outcome, then radiating back out to the authorities that support it. Real perspective projection, lit additively in the brand palette, on transparent backgrounds. They sit behind the interface and drift on a slow parallax, so the page has depth without a single piece of clip art.

/* the imagery is generated, not sourced. perspective-projected
   nodes, additive glow, brand palette, transparent background. */
def render(structure):
  project 3D nodes -> 2D with perspective + depth sort
  splat  additive gaussians: bronze facts, cyan outcome
  tonemap filmic curve, alpha from luminance -> PNG

/* placed behind the UI, drifting on pointer + scroll parallax,
   fully disabled under prefers-reduced-motion. */
06 — How I worked

Audit first, then build the system.

I did not start with a moodboard. I audited the live site from four points of view, inventoried every page and its bugs, and mapped the competitive set. Then I set the direction and proved it the only way that removes doubt in a build-stage company: in working code, with real tokens, accessible contrast, and motion that respects reduced-motion. I do not just make things look good. I build the systems that make good work repeatable.

Four-point-of-view audit
Investor, litigator, engineer, and first-time visitor. Each one finds a different gap, and the page has to answer all four.
Page inventory and SEO pass
Every nav item read end to end. Orphan pages, slugs, and title tags fixed before a single pixel moved.
Competitor deep-dive
A close read of Legora and Harvey to find the wedge Donblas can own, then a positioning line to claim it.
Coded, not mocked
A single self-contained homepage in real HTML and CSS. Design tokens, AA contrast on dark, reduced-motion built in.
