02About
I don't just make things look good. I build what lets good work happen without me in the room.

I'm a designer who ended up running the whole creative function. Fifteen years of UI/UX and product design across gaming, technology and lifestyle: eight years at Telltale Games leading interface design on the Game of the Year winning Walking Dead, then product design across benefits, live broadcast and wellness, then VP of Design & Creative at Sommsation from 2020 to 2026, building the creative function from zero and shipping a full e-commerce platform end to end. I work at the intersection of brand, product and operations.

On the side I'm building Stead, a project of my own that I design end to end and prototype in working code. I consult for companies that need senior design and creative leadership without the full-time hire. I have also taught design at the university level, which remains the best stress test I have ever run on my own thinking. Every concept you can't explain clearly is a concept you don't fully understand yet.

03 — The Approach

Creative, brand and marketing as operable systems.

01

Brand and creative as infrastructure.

I treat brand and creative as a system that can be designed, documented and installed. The thinking is the product. Everything else is evidence it works.

02

Craft you can ship.

Everything I direct, I can build. Wireframes to polished UI, design systems to working prototypes. Fifteen years of interface craft means the direction never floats free of the thing being made.

03

The 10-phase operating system.

Foundation through scale: positioning, identity, voice, digital presence, MarTech, audience, launch, demand gen, investor readiness and governance. Defined inputs, outputs and costed deliverables at every phase. Tested across four companies at four different growth stages.

04

Built to outlive me.

The work I'm proudest of is the work that holds up after I step back. Design systems that travel without a tour guide. Brand frameworks that stay consistent in someone else's hands. Documentation good enough to be implemented, not just admired.
